# Sales deck + transcript → website: what to use, where, and what not to claim

**Sources:** `OBG SALES Training.pdf` (33 slides) · `One_Bonsai_Gulf_Sales_Training_Transcript.pdf` (Ivan, leadership, ~21k words)
**Date:** 10 September 2026

The original audit's biggest AEO finding was that the site contains **almost no extractable facts** — one number across the whole domain ("1,600 staff"). That is why answer engines have nothing to quote. These two documents contain roughly forty. This maps them to pages.

---

## ⚠️ Read this before publishing anything below

Four accuracy problems. Getting these wrong is worse than having no content.

### 1. Nike, Deloitte, Toyota and Port of Antwerp are **not** OneBonsai Gulf clients

They are **OneBonsai Belgium's**. The transcript is explicit: *"We can use their client list — a reference list of them is something that's also standing behind us."* That is a reference, not a customer relationship.

**Never write:** "Our clients include Nike and Toyota."
**Write:** "Delivered by our parent company OneBonsai, whose work includes the first digital twin of a Nike warehouse."

A procurement lead who checks this and finds you claimed someone else's client loses the deal and the trust. Attribution costs one clause.

### 2. Cleveland Clinic is not a client

Transcript: *"We had conversations with Cleveland Clinic about creating an avatar…"* — conversations. **Do not publish this at all.** There is no safe phrasing for a prospect conversation on a public page.

### 3. The case-study count contradicts itself

Deck slide 3 says **70+ case studies, 49+ clients**. Transcript says **"more than 100 use cases, more than 100 realized large-scale products."** Pick one, source it, and use it everywhere. I would use the deck's figures — they are more conservative and more likely to be the checked ones.

### 4. Product names differ between the two documents

The transcript is an auto-transcription and garbles them. **Use the deck spellings:** Tarteeb, Marengo, MedHub, Reqilo, Vyonix, FitHub. (Transcript renders these as "Tative, Metahub, Requilo, Vionix" — ignore.) Same for **Simplify Suite** (`simplifysuite.io`), which the transcript calls "SimpliPhi".

## Three service pages that should exist and don't

The deck describes capabilities the website does not sell.

### 1. `/services/ai-experts-on-demand` — **the biggest gap**

Deck capability 3 + transcript §10. An entire service line with no page: a curated network of **50+ AI and IT specialists**, project-based, fractional, embedded or full delivery team. Named roles: AI governance specialists, LLM and GenAI developers, AI agent developers, data engineers, MLOps specialists.

The sales trigger is stated verbatim: *"We want to implement AI, but we don't have the internal expertise or resources."* That is a page headline and an FAQ question already written.

**Note:** the About page already has an "AI talent & expertise" section saying exactly this. It has no page behind it.

### 2. `/services/uae-market-entry`

Deck capability 5. GCC market entry via the **Masdar City partnership** — legal entity setup, licensing, visa proceedings, local partners, representation. This is a distinct, high-value service with an institutional partner behind it, and it is invisible on the site.

### 3. `/services/digital-twins-simulation`

`digital twin` measures **720/mo** — the largest informational term in the whole keyword set, and section H already flagged it as the strongest article opportunity. The deck has two concrete examples: oil rig worker effectiveness and pump capacity modelling (1,000 → 3,000 L/hr with storage bottleneck prediction), plus the Nike warehouse. Currently the site mentions digital twins only as an accordion bullet.

## What this fixes

The first audit said: *"'1,600 staff and students' is the only number on the whole site. Add engagement durations, team size, founding year, number of deployments, sectors served — figures are what get quoted."*

This material supplies all of it. Publishing it moves the site from **Experience: strong / Expertise: weak** to strong on both — the KU Leuven partnership in particular is exactly the third-party credential the Expertise pillar was missing.

**One caveat that has not changed:** none of this substitutes for a named human author on the articles, or for the missing phone number and street address. Those remain the two open E-E-A-T items.
